You're looking at 374 signals. One is beating SPY. The rest are visible, ranked, and honest about their failures.

This is what happens when you stop hiding losers.

Most algo leaderboards show you the winners. They hide the middle. They bury the bottom. StockArithm's board shows all 374—ranked two different ways—because the gap between them tells you something real about what's working *right now* versus what worked *over time*.

## The Two Leaderboards: What They Measure

**Force Rank** is your all-time scoreboard. It's based on total return since each signal launched, typically 114 days of live trading. It answers: "Which strategies have made the most money from start to finish?"

Right now, **Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation** is #1 on force rank. It's up 16.06% year-to-date, beating SPY's 10.17% by 589 basis points. It's the only signal in the entire system beating the benchmark on a full-window basis.

**Rolling 30D** is your recent-form leaderboard. It measures the last 30 calendar days of returns, Sharpe ratio, and drawdown. It answers: "What's hot *this month*?"

Same signal—**Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation**—is also #1 on rolling 30D. It returned 16.06% in the last 30 days, outperforming SPY by 1,111 basis points. That's rare alignment.

But alignment is the exception, not the rule.

## The Divergence: When Recent Form Breaks from History

Look at **VIX Fear Rotation**. 

On force rank, it's #372 out of 374. Year-to-date, it's down 1.17%. It's a failure by the long-term measure.

On rolling 30D, it's #3. In the last 30 days, it returned 6.08%, beating SPY by 111 basis points. It's a winner by the recent measure.

That's a rank gap of 369 positions.

This happens because:
- A signal can be broken for months, then suddenly work.
- Market regimes shift. A strategy that lost money in Q1 might thrive in Q2.
- Luck compounds. A few good weeks can vault a chronic underperformer into the top 5.

The board shows both. You see the full history *and* the recent momentum. You're not fooled by either one alone.

## Why This Matters: The Honest Version

If you only looked at force rank, you'd miss that **Chaos Rotation Lab** (ranked #374, down 6.08% YTD) has actually returned 0.18% in the last 30 days and ranks #14 on rolling 30D. It's still a loser overall, but it's not dead. The board lets you see that.

If you only looked at rolling 30D, you'd chase hot signals that have no track record. **Retail Sales Momentum** is #5 on rolling 30D but doesn't appear in the top force-ranked list—it's been running for 114 days and hasn't beaten SPY on a full-window basis. Recent strength doesn't mean durability.

The split forces you to ask better questions:
- Is this signal consistently good, or just lucky this month?
- Did it fail for a reason, or did the market just change?
- Am I chasing momentum, or backing a proven edge?

## How to Use This

When you're evaluating a signal:

1. **Check force rank first.** Has it made money over its full life? If not, why would you trade it?
2. **Check rolling 30D second.** Is it working *now*? If force rank is strong but rolling 30D is weak, the strategy may be breaking. If rolling 30D is strong but force rank is weak, you're looking at a recent hot streak—not a proven edge.
3. **Look at the gap.** A signal with a huge rank divergence is either recovering or deteriorating. Watch it closely.
4. **See the failures.** The board doesn't hide them. That's the feature.

The board is a tool for skeptics. It shows you what's winning, what's losing, and the gap between them. One signal is beating SPY. The other 373 are teaching you what doesn't work—or what doesn't work yet.

That's worth reading.
