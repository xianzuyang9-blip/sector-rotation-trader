# Why StockArithm exists

I built StockArithm because I got tired of watching smart people lose money to bad signals.

Not because they were dumb. Because the signals they were following—the ones everyone talks about, the ones with names and track records and confident Twitter threads—were often just noise dressed up as insight.

I spent years in quant research watching algo after algo fail in real time. Not fail in backtests. Fail when real money was on the line. A strategy would look bulletproof on historical data, then the market would shift three degrees and suddenly it was underwater. The worst part wasn't the losses. It was the *confusion*. Nobody could tell you why it failed. Was it the market regime? The data? The logic? Or just bad luck?

The standard answer was always the same: "Past performance doesn't guarantee future results." True. Useless.

So I started asking a different question: What if we stopped pretending we could predict the future, and instead just measured what's actually working *right now*?

That sounds obvious. It isn't. Most of the financial industry is built on the opposite premise—that if you find the right model, the right factors, the right story, you can lock in returns. Sell that story to clients. Collect fees. Move on.

What I wanted to build was a lab. Not a product. A place where we could run hundreds of different trading ideas in parallel, measure them against a single honest benchmark (the S&P 500), and watch which ones actually beat it. Not in theory. In practice. Every single day.

The lab would be ruthless. No sacred cows. No "this strategy is supposed to work because the academic paper said so." Just: Does it work today? Did it work last month? Is it working right now?

And crucially: What's the honest failure rate?

Today, May 24, 2026, we're running 344 different algorithms across 105 tickers. Only 1 of them is beating the S&P 500 on a force-ranked basis. Over the last 30 days, 4 out of 345 are ahead. That's a 1.2% win rate on the rolling window.

That's not a bug. That's the point.

Most people see those numbers and think the lab is failing. I see it differently. We're finally being honest about what the market actually is: a place where most ideas don't work, most of the time. The value isn't in pretending otherwise. It's in finding the rare ones that do, measuring them precisely, and understanding *why* they work before the market changes and they don't.

Right now, the "Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation" is our top performer—up 13.09% year-to-date, beating SPY by 3.64%. It's been running for 112 days. That's real. But I don't know if it'll still be #1 in 30 days. The market doesn't care about my confidence. It cares about whether the logic still holds.

That's why the lab exists. Not to sell you a strategy. Not to promise you 20% annual returns. Not to make you feel smart about your portfolio.

It exists to show you what's actually working, what's actually failing, and to do it with enough transparency that you can see the difference between signal and luck.

We run the crazy ideas too—the ones based on copper prices, port container volumes, VIX term structure. Some of them work for a few weeks. Most don't. But we measure them all the same way. No special pleading. No "this one's different because it's based on alternative data." Just: Does it beat SPY?

The lab is a mirror. Not a crystal ball.

If you're tired of strategies that sound good in theory but fail in practice, if you want to see what's actually working in real time instead of reading about it in a white paper six months after it stopped working, that's what we're building.
