# Why the Failures Stay Visible

One signal is beating SPY. Three hundred seventy-three are not.

That's the math at StockArithm right now. Out of 374 ranked signals, only the Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation has outperformed the S&P 500 year-to-date. It's up 17.14%. SPY is up 10.76%. The gap is real: 6.38% of alpha.

The other 373 signals? They're visible too. And they're losing.

This isn't a story about the one winner. It's about why we keep the losers in plain sight—and what that tells you about how to think about systematic trading.

## The Leaderboard Never Lies

The bottom of our force-ranked list reads like a graveyard of clever ideas.

The Liquor Store Leading Indicator is down 1.1% year-to-date. Port Container Volume is down 0.94%. The FINRA Dark Pool Signal is down 0.92%. These aren't rounding errors. These are signals that someone built, tested, and deployed with conviction. They're now underperforming SPY by 11–12 percentage points.

The Faber Momentum Rotation—a legitimate, published strategy—is up 8.2% but still trailing SPY by 2.56% of alpha. The Antonacci Dual Momentum Sector Rotation, another established framework, is at 7.14% YTD. Both are ranked in the top five by force rank. Both are losing.

This is the uncomfortable part of quantitative trading that marketing decks skip over. Most strategies don't work most of the time. The ones that do work are exceptions, not the rule.

## Why We Don't Hide the Failures

You could build a leaderboard that only shows winners. You could cherry-pick the 30-day rolling window where everything looks good. You could exclude the signals that haven't run long enough to matter. You could redefine "beating SPY" to mean something more forgiving.

We don't.

The reason is simple: if you're going to trust a signal with real money, you need to see the full picture. You need to know that 373 other approaches failed. You need to see the Chaos Rotation Lab ranked dead last on force rank (374th) while somehow posting a 0.8% return in the last 30 days. You need to understand that divergence—that gap between long-term failure and short-term luck—because that's where real risk lives.

The failures stay visible because they're data. They're evidence. They're the ground truth of what works and what doesn't in a market that doesn't care about your hypothesis.

## The One That Works

The Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation is beating SPY. It's the only one. It's up 17.14% year-to-date against SPY's 10.76%. It has a 30-day Sharpe ratio of 6.73. Its maximum drawdown in the last month was 3.5%—manageable, real, honest.

It's also the simplest idea on the board. Monthly rebalancing. Sector rotation. No dark pools. No liquor stores. No chaos.

That's not a coincidence. It's a pattern. The strategies that work tend to be the ones that don't try to outsmart the market with exotic data. They work because they're robust. They work because they're boring. They work because they don't break when the market changes.

And they work *despite* the fact that 373 other signals are working against them, competing for the same capital, the same attention, the same belief.

## What This Means for You

If you're building or evaluating trading signals, the lesson is this: **the leaderboard is the product.**

Not the top performer. Not the story. The whole list. The winners and the 373 losers. The signals that looked good in backtests but failed in live trading. The ones that worked for 30 days and then stopped. The ones that are still running but underwater.

This is what real signal evaluation looks like. It's not glamorous. It's not a pitch. It's a mirror.

If you want to understand what works in markets, you have to be willing to see what doesn't. You have to accept that most ideas fail. You have to respect the one that doesn't—not because it's flashy, but because it's rare.

The failures stay visible because they're the context. Without them, the one winner is just a number. With them, it's evidence.
