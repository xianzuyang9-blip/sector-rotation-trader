# What a named signal teaches us

StockArithm is a system that ranks trading algorithms against each other in real time. We run 403 different signals across 105 stocks, from simple momentum rotations to experimental indicators based on everything from dark pool activity to liquor store foot traffic. Every day, we measure which ones beat the S&P 500 and which ones don't. The point isn't to sell you a trading system. It's to show you what actually works, what fails, and what that gap tells us about how markets behave.

Today I want to walk through one named signal—the Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation—not because it's a recommendation, but because it's a teaching example. Right now it's our top performer. But the real lesson isn't in the win. It's in what we learn by comparing it to everything else that's running.

As of June 4, 2026, the Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation has returned 13.68% year-to-date. The S&P 500 returned 11.12% over the same period. That's a 2.56% alpha—a real, measurable outperformance. Over the last 30 days, it returned 9.49% while the broad market returned 4.60%. It's been running for 121 days and has beaten SPY on both the full-window force rank and the rolling 30-day leaderboard.

The equity curve sits at $113,683.92 on a $100,000 starting balance.

Those are the facts. No interpretation. No story. Just what happened.

## Why this matters more than the number

Here's what makes this signal worth studying: it's simple. The name tells you almost everything. It rotates between holdings once a month based on quantified criteria. No dark pools. No sentiment scraping. No exotic data. Just a mechanical rule applied consistently.

In a universe of 403 algorithms, only 1 is beating SPY right now. That's 0.25% of all signals. Over the last 30 days, 3 out of 404 beat the market. The Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation is one of them.

But here's the uncomfortable part: simplicity doesn't guarantee durability. We're running 403 signals because we don't know which ones will work tomorrow. The ones that work today often don't work next month. That's not a failure of the system. That's the market.

## The contrast: what doesn't work

To understand why the Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation matters, look at what's failing.

The Electricity Consumption signal is ranked 403rd—dead last. It's down 6.61% year-to-date while SPY is up 11.12%. That's a -17.74% alpha. The signal was built on a reasonable hypothesis: electricity usage correlates with economic activity, and economic activity drives stock returns. It didn't work.

The Liquor Store Leading Indicator is ranked 401st. Down 2.99% YTD. The Consumer Sentiment signal is ranked 399th. Down 2.29% YTD. The FINRA Dark Pool Signal, which sounds sophisticated, is ranked 400th. Down 2.57% YTD.

These aren't bad ideas. They're just ideas that didn't predict price movement in this market, in this window. That's the core insight: a plausible hypothesis is not the same as a working signal.

## The hidden lesson: recent strength doesn't predict future strength

There's a third category worth examining: the notable divergences. These are signals that rank much higher on the rolling 30-day leaderboard than they do on the full-window force rank.

Algo Biscotti (Unconditional Loyalty) is ranked 398th overall but 14th over the last 30 days. That's a 384-rank gap. It's returned 0.86% in the last month while sitting at -2.2% year-to-date. The Daily Rise In Freightlogistics Google Trends signal is ranked 388th overall but 22nd in the last 30 days.

What does this tell us? Recent performance and durable performance are not the same thing. A signal can look hot for a month and still be a long-term loser. The Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation doesn't have this problem—it's ranked #1 both ways. But most signals do.

This is why we track both metrics. The full-window force rank tells you what actually works over time. The rolling 30-day rank tells you what's working right now. When they diverge sharply, you're looking at either a signal that's just gotten lucky or one that's finally turning a corner. You can't know which until more time passes.

## What you should take away

The Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation is working right now. It's simple. It's transparent. It's beating the market by a meaningful margin. But it's one signal out of 403, and the fact that it's winning today doesn't mean it will win tomorrow.

The real value of tracking named signals isn't picking winners. It's learning to distinguish between:

- Signals that work consistently (rare)
- Signals that work in specific market conditions (common)
- Signals that got lucky (very common)
- Signals that are genuinely broken (also common)

The Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation has shown consistency across both time windows and a defensive market. That's worth noting. But consistency over 121 days is not the same as consistency over years. The only way to know is to keep watching.
