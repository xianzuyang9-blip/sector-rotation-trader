# What a Named Signal Teaches Us

Out of 403 signals running today, one is beating SPY. Just one.

That's not a marketing line. It's a fact that should make anyone building or trading algorithmic systems sit down and think hard about what works, what doesn't, and why the gap between them matters more than the winners themselves.

The signal that's winning is called **Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation**. It's been running for 121 days. It's up 13.68% year-to-date while SPY is up 11.12%—a 2.56% alpha advantage. Over the last 30 days, it's returned 9.49% against SPY's 4.6%. The math is clean. The results are real.

But here's what's more instructive: the 402 signals that aren't beating SPY are still visible. They're ranked. They're measured. And they're failing in ways that teach us something.

## The Winner: Simplicity Over Complexity

Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation does one thing: it rotates holdings on a monthly schedule. No machine learning. No sentiment scraping. No dark pool analysis. No liquor store leading indicators.

It works because it's rebalancing systematically into sectors and holdings that have momentum, and it's doing it on a predictable cadence. The Sharpe ratio over 30 days is 2.94—solid risk-adjusted returns. The maximum drawdown in that window was only 4.03%. It's not flashy. It's not clever. It's just consistent.

Compare that to the second-ranked signal by year-to-date return: **Algo Baileymol (Chaos Monger)**, which is down 2.96% against SPY. Same 121-day track record. Same market conditions. Different approach. Different outcome.

The lesson isn't that monthly rotation always wins. The lesson is that *simplicity survived*. When you're competing against 403 different hypotheses about how markets work, the ones that don't require constant tuning, exotic data, or perfect timing tend to hold up better.

## The Failures: Visible and Specific

At the bottom of the leaderboard, we see **Electricity Consumption** ranked 403rd, down 6.61% year-to-date. Below it in the 30-day window: **Liquor Store Leading Indicator** (down 2.99% YTD), **FINRA Dark Pool Signal** (down 2.57%), and **Consumer Sentiment** (down 2.29%).

These aren't hypothetical failures. These are real signals that someone built, tested, and deployed. They had a thesis. They had data. They had conviction. And they lost to a simple monthly rotation.

Why does this matter? Because the failures stay visible. You can see exactly how much they underperformed. You can see the drawdowns. You can see that Consumer Sentiment had a Sharpe ratio of -4.6 over the last month—meaning it lost money with high volatility. That's the worst kind of failure.

Most trading systems hide their losers. They get archived, forgotten, or quietly shut down. Here, they're ranked alongside the winners. That transparency is uncomfortable. It's also essential.

## The Divergences: Recency vs. History

There's a third category worth examining: signals that are terrible over the full window but decent over the last 30 days.

**Algo Biscotti (Unconditional Loyalty)** is ranked 398th overall but 14th over the last month. It's down 2.2% year-to-date but up 0.86% in the last 30 days. That's a 384-rank gap between its full-window performance and its recent performance.

This is where the real diagnostic work happens. Is Biscotti turning around? Is it a false signal? Is it just noise? You can't know without looking at the data. But you *can* see the divergence. You can ask the question. And that's the point.

The signals that show the biggest rank gaps between long-term and short-term performance are the ones worth investigating. They're either adapting to new market conditions, or they're about to fail harder. Either way, they're telling you something.

## What This Teaches Us

The StockArithm system runs 403 signals across 105 tickers. On any given day, most of them are wrong. The distribution is brutal: one winner, 402 losers. But that distribution is also honest.

If you're building trading systems, this is the environment you're actually in. Not the backtested environment where your signal works 60% of the time. The real environment where you're competing against hundreds of other hypotheses, and most of them fail.

The signals that survive aren't the ones with the cleverest data sources. They're the ones that:

- **Reduce complexity** instead of adding it
- **Rebalance systematically** instead of trying to time entries
- **Measure honestly** instead of hiding drawdowns
- **Adapt slowly** instead of overfitting to recent noise

Quantified Simple Monthly Rotation isn't winning because it's revolutionary. It's winning because it's robust. It works in multiple market regimes. It doesn't require perfect execution. It doesn't break when the data source hiccups.

## The Real Takeaway

The fact that one signal is beating SPY out of 403 isn't a failure of the system. It's a feature. It means the system is actually testing hypotheses instead of confirming them. It means the losers are visible. It means you can learn from failure.

If you're trading, building systems, or just trying to understand how markets actually work, spend less time looking for the next clever signal. Spend more time understanding why the simple ones survive.

The data is public. The rankings are updated daily. The failures are visible.

That's the real lesson.
